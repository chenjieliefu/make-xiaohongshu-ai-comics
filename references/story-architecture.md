# Story architecture

Use eight pages by default. Adapt the count to the concept rather than padding.

1. **Cover:** name the concept and open a curiosity gap.
2. **Relatable friction:** show the user problem or surprising behavior.
3. **Definition:** give one accurate plain-language sentence and a simple model.
4. **Mechanism or real work:** break the concept into three to five parts.
5. **Concrete comparison:** before/after, wrong/right, input/output, or analogy/reality.
6. **Evaluation and limits:** show how to test success and where the method stops.
7. **Misconceptions:** correct two or three myths with necessary qualifiers.
8. **Takeaway:** provide a small checklist and invite the next concept.

## Page contract

```markdown
### Page NN — <purpose>
Claim: <one factual sentence>
Headline: <verbatim text>
Body: <verbatim text>
Labels: <verbatim labels>
Visual metaphor: <one concrete original scene>
Characters: <who appears and why>
Style application: <layout or rendering note>
Accuracy note: <qualifier that must survive>
```

## Copy budget

- Cover: one headline and one hook.
- Interior: one headline, one sentence or dialogue exchange, up to five labels.
- Closing: one qualification plus a compact actionable checklist.
- Prefer fewer than 70 Chinese characters per page.

## Tone

Use curiosity, discovery, and testing. Put humor in the situation or metaphor, not in a false definition. End with useful agency rather than hype.
