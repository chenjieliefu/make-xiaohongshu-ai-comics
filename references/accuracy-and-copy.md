# Knowledge accuracy and copy rules

## Research hierarchy

Prefer official documentation or standards, original research and system cards, then first-party engineering explanations. Use high-quality secondary sources only when primary material is unavailable.

Record source URLs and access dates. For a general AI concept, corroborate the core definition with two primary sources when practical.

## Claim discipline

- Distinguish a technical method from a job title or marketing label.
- Separate instructions, context, examples, tools, model choice, retrieval, fine-tuning, and evaluation.
- State prerequisites and cases the method cannot solve by itself.
- Avoid anthropomorphism implying consciousness, intent, certainty, or human understanding.
- Do not turn possibility into certainty or a task-specific observation into a universal rule.
- Preserve qualifiers such as “can,” “often,” “for this task,” and “when tested against…”.
- Never invent numbers, benchmarks, salaries, adoption rates, quotes, or guaranteed results.

## Simplification test

Before approving comic copy, ask whether shortening:

1. changed the subject;
2. removed a condition;
3. turned a metaphor into a literal mechanism;
4. implied the model knows facts that were never supplied;
5. hid a meaningful limitation.

Rewrite when any answer is yes.

## Text QA

Compare rendered text line by line against the storyboard. Check simplified Chinese, punctuation, Arabic numerals, English capitalization, full-width symbols, and technical terms. Treat corrupted text as a draft even when the art is attractive.
